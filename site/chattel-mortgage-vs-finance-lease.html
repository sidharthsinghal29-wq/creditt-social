Chattel mortgage or finance lease: which suits your business?

4 min read

Both structures let your business use a vehicle or piece of equipment without paying the full price upfront. The difference is who owns the asset, and that changes what happens at the end.

Chattel mortgage

You buy the asset and own it from day one. The lender takes a mortgage over it as security and releases it when the loan is repaid. Some loans include a final balloon payment, which lowers the regular repayments but leaves a lump sum due at the end.

Finance lease

The lender owns the asset and you pay to use it for an agreed term. At the end you may be able to return it, extend the lease or buy it, depending on the agreement. Some businesses prefer this when equipment dates quickly.

What to ask your accountant

Tax and GST treatment can differ between the two, and it depends on your GST registration, accounting basis and whether you want the asset on your balance sheet. We can't give tax advice, so ask your accountant which suits your situation before you sign.

What to compare

Compare total cost over the full term, not just the monthly repayment. Look at fees, any balloon payment, early payout terms and what happens at the end of the contract.

This is general information, not tax or financial advice.
