First home buyer guide for NSW (2026)

The short answer: in NSW in 2026, an eligible first home buyer can pay no stamp duty on a home up to $800,000 (reduced duty up to $1 million), buy with a 5% deposit and no lenders mortgage insurance on a Sydney property up to $1.5 million, and get a $10,000 grant if the home is new and within the grant's caps. Here's how each one works, who qualifies, and how to combine them.

At a glance: NSW first home buyer support (November 2026)

  • Stamp duty (Revenue NSW): no duty up to $800,000, reduced duty above $800,000 and under $1,000,000. Vacant land: no duty up to $350,000, reduced under $450,000.
  • 5% Deposit Scheme (Australian Government): 5% deposit, no LMI. Price cap $1.5 million in Sydney, Newcastle, Lake Macquarie and the Illawarra, and $800,000 elsewhere in NSW. No income caps, no limit on places.
  • First Home Owner Grant (Revenue NSW): $10,000 for a new home up to $600,000, or a house-and-land or owner-builder project up to $750,000.

1. Stamp duty: the First Home Buyers Assistance Scheme

Eligible first home buyers in NSW pay no transfer (stamp) duty on new or existing homes up to $800,000, and a reduced amount on homes priced above $800,000 and under $1,000,000. The concession shrinks as the price rises, and at $1,000,000 or more full duty applies. For vacant land you intend to build a home on, there's no duty up to $350,000 and a concession under $450,000.

To qualify, generally you (and anyone buying with you) must be at least 18, never have owned residential property in Australia, and not have received the exemption before, and at least one buyer must be an Australian citizen or permanent resident. You must move in within 12 months of settlement and live there continuously for at least 12 months. The First Home Buyer Choice option (paying annual land tax instead of duty) closed to new purchases on 1 July 2023.

2. The 5% Deposit Scheme

The Australian Government 5% Deposit Scheme (previously called the Home Guarantee Scheme, run by Housing Australia) lets eligible buyers purchase with a 5% deposit without paying lenders mortgage insurance (LMI). The government guarantees up to 15% of the property value to the lender, so the loan is treated as if you had a 20% deposit.

Since 1 October 2025 there are no income caps and no limit on places. The NSW price caps are $1.5 million for Sydney and the regional centres of Newcastle, Lake Macquarie and the Illawarra, and $800,000 for the rest of the state. The home must be one you'll live in, not an investment. You apply through a participating lender, and a broker can compare which ones suit you. The regional version of the scheme has been folded into this main guarantee.

3. The First Home Owner Grant (new homes)

A $10,000 grant is available to eligible buyers of a new home, including off-the-plan and substantially renovated homes, priced up to $600,000. If you're buying land and signing a building contract, or building as an owner-builder, the combined cost must be no more than $750,000. It doesn't apply to existing homes. The same 12-month move-in and 12-month residence rules apply. If you're building, see our construction loans page for how progress payments work.

4. Combining the schemes: a Sydney example

Say you're buying an existing $780,000 apartment in Sydney. If you're eligible, you could pay no stamp duty (it's under $800,000), use the 5% Deposit Scheme to buy with a $39,000 deposit and no LMI (it's under the $1.5 million cap), but you wouldn't get the First Home Owner Grant because the home isn't new. At $1.1 million, you could still use the 5% Deposit Scheme, but full stamp duty would apply. This is general illustration only, so check your own eligibility before you rely on it.

5. Your deposit and the real upfront costs

  • Deposit: from 5% with the scheme, 20% to avoid LMI without it, or somewhere in between with LMI.
  • Stamp duty: possibly nil or reduced, as above.
  • Conveyancing, building and pest (or strata) inspections, and loan application or settlement fees.
  • A buffer for moving costs, insurance and the first few months of repayments.

6. How much can you borrow?

Lenders look at your income, expenses, existing debts (including credit card limits and HECS-HELP) and deposit, and test repayments at a rate higher than the actual rate. A smaller deposit doesn't change that test, so the scheme helps with the deposit, not with borrowing power. See how much can I borrow and try our borrowing power calculator.
