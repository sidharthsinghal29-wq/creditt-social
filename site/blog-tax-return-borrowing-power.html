How your tax return affects your borrowing power

If you're self-employed, your tax return is your payslip as far as a lender is concerned. How your returns are prepared, and when they're lodged, has a direct effect on how much you can borrow. Here's what lenders look at and what you can do about it.

What lenders look at

For self-employed borrowers, most lenders ask for the last two years of tax returns and the matching ATO notices of assessment. Many use the most recent year if income is rising, or an average if it has dropped. Employees usually only need payslips, but tax returns matter for them too when income includes bonuses, overtime, rent or a side business.

Lower taxable income can mean lower borrowing power

Lenders start from your taxable income. Every deduction that reduces your tax bill also reduces the income a lender sees. That's not a reason to give up legitimate deductions, but if a loan is coming up in the next year or two, it's worth planning the trade-off with your accountant before the return is lodged.

Add-backs: why lender choice matters

Some lenders will add back certain items to your income, such as depreciation, one-off expenses, interest on loans being refinanced, or super contributions above the minimum. Policies differ between lenders, so the same tax return can support quite different loan amounts. A broker who reads your returns properly can point you to lenders whose policy suits them.

Lodge on time and keep ATO debts under control

Overdue returns limit you to fewer lenders, and many lenders won't proceed with an unmanaged ATO debt. If you owe the ATO, an agreed payment plan that you're keeping up with is viewed far more favourably than an unpaid balance.

Rental and investment income

Rental income shown in your return is usually counted, but most lenders only use a portion of it to allow for vacancies and costs. Negative gearing benefits may be treated differently from lender to lender.

A simple checklist before you apply

  • Have the last two years of returns lodged, with notices of assessment on hand.
  • Keep BAS lodgements up to date.
  • Put any ATO debt on a formal payment plan.
  • Talk to your accountant and broker before lodging if a loan is coming up soon.
